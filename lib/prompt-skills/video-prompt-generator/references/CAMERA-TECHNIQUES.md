# Camera Techniques Reference

Modern director-controllable video models respond strongly to specific camera language. This reference covers cinematic movements and their narrative purposes — universal vocabulary that lands across all current models.

## Speed Modifiers

Pair any movement keyword with a speed modifier for precise pacing control. These are universal — every modern video model honors them.

| Modifier | Effect | Use For |
|----------|--------|---------|
| `slow` | Builds tension, intimacy, weight | Emotional beats, reveals, dread |
| `medium` | Natural cinematic pace | General coverage, dialogue |
| `fast` | Energy, urgency, surprise | Action, transitions, panic |
| `gradual` | Progressive shift across the duration | Reveals over time, emotional drift |

**Examples:**
- `slow push-in` — tightening on a face, building dread
- `medium dolly forward` — natural cinematic approach
- `fast whip-pan` — kinetic transition between subjects
- `gradual pull-back` — reveal over time, emotional distance opening up

Combine with any camera movement: `slow arc`, `fast tilt down`, `gradual orbit`, `medium tracking`, etc.


## Shot Types

### Distance-Based Shots
| Shot | Description | Narrative Use |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Extreme Wide / Establishing | Shows entire location | Set context, show scale |
| Wide | Full body + environment | Action sequences, geography |
| Medium | Waist up | Dialogue, general coverage |
| Medium Close-up | Chest up | Emotional dialogue |
| Close-up | Face fills frame | Emotion, reaction, detail |
| Extreme Close-up / Macro | Single feature (eyes, hands) | Tension, intimacy, detail |

### Angle-Based Shots
| Shot | Description | Narrative Use |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Eye Level | Camera at subject height | Neutral, relatable |
| Low Angle | Camera below subject | Power, heroism, dominance |
| High Angle | Camera above subject | Vulnerability, smallness |
| Dutch / Canted | Tilted horizon | Unease, disorientation |
| Bird's Eye | Directly overhead | God's view, pattern reveal |
| Worm's Eye | Extreme low, looking up | Extreme power, monumentality |

### Position-Based Shots
| Shot | Description | Narrative Use |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) | Behind one character toward another | Dialogue, confrontation |
| POV (Point of View) | Through character's eyes | Immersion, suspense |
| Two-Shot | Two subjects in frame | Relationship, dialogue |
| Profile Shot | Side view of subject | Contemplation, stoicism |
| Three-Quarter | 45-degree angle to subject | Flattering, dynamic |

## Camera Movements

### Dolly Movements
Physical camera movement through space.

| Movement | Description | Narrative Use |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| Dolly In / Push In | Camera moves toward subject | Build tension, increase intimacy |
| Dolly Out / Pull Back | Camera moves away from subject | Reveal context, create distance |
| Dolly Alongside / Tracking | Camera moves parallel to subject | Follow action, journey |

**Example:** `The camera begins a very slow push-in, moving steadily closer to the player's face. This movement builds anticipation.`

### Pan and Tilt
Camera rotates on fixed position.

| Movement | Description | Narrative Use |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| Pan Left/Right | Horizontal rotation | Survey scene, follow action |
| Tilt Up/Down | Vertical rotation | Reveal height, show scale |
| Whip Pan | Very fast pan | Transition, energy, surprise |

**Example:** `Camera pans slowly from the window to reveal the woman standing in the doorway.`

### Complex Movements
| Movement | Description | Narrative Use |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| Arc / Orbit | Camera circles around subject | Dynamic reveal, drama |
| Crane Up/Down | Vertical camera lift | Dramatic reveal, scope |
| Jib Shot | Sweeping vertical movement | Elegance, production value |
| Steadicam / Floating | Smooth handheld follow | Intimacy with movement |
| Handheld | Intentional shake | Realism, tension, documentary |

**Example:** `Use a very slow 180-degree arc shot that orbits both of them. The slow, continuous movement builds dramatic tension.`

### Zoom vs Dolly
Zoom changes focal length (unnatural look). Dolly physically moves camera (natural perspective).

**Zoom Example:** `Crash zoom into the character's face` - Stylized, unnatural
**Dolly Example:** `Camera dollies in slowly to the character's face` - Cinematic, natural

## Combining Movements

### Movement + Shot Type
```
A slow dolly push-in from medium shot to close-up
```

### Movement + Duration
```
The camera tracks alongside her for 5 seconds, then gently pushes into a close-up
```

### Movement + Subject Action
```
Tracking shot following from the side as she walks, camera matching her pace
```

## Camera Narrative Intent

Match camera to emotional purpose:

### Building Tension
- Slow push-in
- Tightening frame
- Reducing movement
- Static with slight drift

### Creating Energy
- Fast tracking
- Whip pans
- Handheld shake
- Quick cuts (in multi-shot)

### Establishing Intimacy
- Close-ups
- Slow movements
- Eye-level framing
- Shallow depth of field

### Showing Power
- Low angles
- Slow tilt up
- Wide lens close to subject
- Subject moving toward camera

### Creating Unease
- Dutch angles
- Unpredictable movement
- Wide lens distortion
- Off-center framing

## Lens Language

These function as stylistic cues, not actual optical parameters:

| Lens | Visual Effect | Use For |
|------|---------------|---------|
| 35mm | Natural, documentary feel | Dialogue, general |
| 50mm | Most natural perspective | Portraits, clean look |
| 85mm | Compressed, flattering | Beauty, close-ups |
| Wide angle (24mm or less) | Distortion, more context | Establishing, action |
| Telephoto (100mm+) | Compression, isolation | Drama, sports |

**Example:** `Shot on 35mm film with shallow focus and glowing bokeh behind them`

## Focus Techniques

| Technique | Description | Use For |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Shallow DOF | Subject sharp, background blurred | Isolation, intimacy |
| Deep Focus | Everything sharp | Context, environment |
| Rack Focus | Shift focus between subjects | Reveal, connection |
| Snap Focus | Quick focus change | Surprise, energy |
| Soft Focus | Intentionally dreamy | Romance, memory |

**Example:** `Background blurs away as the camera's focus sharpens on her determined expression`

## Transition Keywords

For multi-shot sequences:

| Keyword | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Cut to | Hard transition to next shot |
| Shift focus to | Rack focus within same shot |
| Pull back to reveal | Dolly out showing more |
| Push in to | Dolly in for emphasis |
| Match cut | Visual similarity bridges shots |
| Dissolve to | Soft blend between shots |

## Complete Camera Prompt Examples

### Emotional Close-up
```
Static tripod camera in narrow neon-lit ramen shop, condensation fogs the window, couple sits side by side under flickering magenta sign, steam rising from bowls as they eat noodles in slow synchronized rhythm, broth splattering gently, their faces softly illuminated by red neon glow, shot on 35mm film with shallow focus and glowing bokeh behind them.
```

### Action Tracking
```
Handheld tracking shot follows the runner through the crowded market, camera stays at shoulder height, slight natural bounce, moving between stalls as she navigates the chaos.
```

### Dramatic Reveal
```
Camera begins on extreme close-up of hands gripping a steering wheel. Slow pull back reveals the driver's tense face, continue pulling back through rain-streaked window to wide shot of car stopped on empty highway.
```

### Power Shot
```
Low angle shot looking up at the knight standing in the field. Slow tilt up from boots to helmet, ending on the sky. The movement makes the knight appear heroic and powerful.
```
